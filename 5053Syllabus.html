Content

The material in this course is outstanding!

We will be studying the representation theory of the symmetric group. This topic combines combinatorics, abstract algebra, and linear algebra to understand how representations of the symmetric group break into irreducible components. Students who complete this course will be well prepared to enter a PhD program in algebraic combinatorics.

I have written complete notes for this course. We will be following our course notes closely, and the content of this course is the content of the notes. Depending on where our discussions lead us, we may discuss bonus topics not found in the notes, in which case I will make clear which topics cannot be found in the notes.

If the notes are not enough or you would like another perspective on these topics, excellent second references include

  1. The Symmetric Group: Representations, Combinatorial Algorithms, and Symmetric Functions by Bruce E. Sagan. This is a wonderful book!
  2. The Representation theory of the Symmetric groups by G. D. James. This is an older reference, but still quite good.

If these two references are not sufficient for you, then ask me and I can recommend more texts on the subject.

Academic honesty

No electronics (including calculators) are allowed on assessments.

For the exercise sets, collaboration with classmates and professors is highly encouraged! Computer algebra systems can be used on the exercise sets freely.

Heavy use of AI tools such as ChatGPT to complete the exercise sets will seriously hamper your mathematical development, so please use such tools very lightly (if at all).
